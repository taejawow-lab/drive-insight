## Quick decision table

| Situation | Safer choice | Mistake to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Hail begins lightly | Increase following distance, reduce speed smoothly, and look for a legal safe stop | Braking hard or swerving toward a shoulder |
| Visibility drops | Leave the traffic lane only when you can do it predictably | Stopping under an overpass in a live lane |
| Windshield is struck | Keep seat belts on, face away from glass when stopped, and wait for the core storm to pass | Driving faster to escape while visibility is poor |
| After the storm | Check glass, lights, tires, and route before re-entering traffic | Pulling back into traffic with damaged visibility |

![setup visual](/images/illustrations/di-hailstorm-pullover-20260625-01.png)

## 1. Decide before the windshield fails
Hail while driving is a visibility and traffic-position problem first. The safest response is smooth speed reduction,
extra following distance, and early scanning for a legal safe place to stop. Sudden braking, U-turns, and shoulder dives
can be more dangerous than the hail itself.

![supporting visual 2](/images/illustrations/di-hailstorm-pullover-20260625-02.png)

## 2. Choose a stop that does not create a second hazard
A sheltered stop is useful only if reaching it is lawful and predictable. Do not block a live lane, stop under an
overpass where traffic must merge around you, or follow another driver into a risky space. If you stop, keep seat belts
on and make the vehicle visible without stepping into traffic.

![supporting visual 3](/images/illustrations/di-hailstorm-pullover-20260625-03.png)

## 3. Protect passengers while waiting
Passengers should stay away from glass as much as practical once the car is stopped. Keep phones charged, avoid opening
doors into traffic, and wait until the most intense hail and wind pass. If emergency instructions or officers direct
traffic, follow them over any general checklist.

![supporting visual 4](/images/illustrations/di-hailstorm-pullover-20260625-04.png)

## 4. Inspect the vehicle before moving again
Before moving again, check whether the windshield, mirrors, lights, wipers, and tires still support safe driving. A
cracked windshield, disabled wipers, or debris on the road may require roadside assistance rather than continuing the
trip.

![supporting visual 5](/images/illustrations/di-hailstorm-pullover-20260625-05.png)

## 5. Use official weather information, not guesses
This article preserves reader trust by avoiding fake dashboard screenshots, invented sign text, and legal overclaims.
The useful part is the decision sequence: slow, find a lawful stop, protect passengers, inspect, then rejoin traffic
only when conditions allow.
