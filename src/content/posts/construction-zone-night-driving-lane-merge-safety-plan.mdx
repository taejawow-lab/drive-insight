Night construction zones compress several hazards into a few seconds: glare, temporary lane markings, narrower shoulders, workers near traffic, impatient merges, and drivers who notice the pattern late. This guide was checked on 2026-06-17 against NHTSA, FHWA, work-zone safety, and AAA night-driving resources. It is not legal advice; obey posted signs, officers, flaggers, and local law first, and never use a checklist as permission to drive faster than conditions allow.

## Decision table

| Situation | Safer choice | Mistake to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Lane closure appears ahead | Signal early and merge smoothly | Racing to the taper then forcing in |
| Temporary markings conflict | Follow active cones, signs, and workers | Assuming old lane paint is correct |
| Glare or rain reduces visibility | Increase following distance and reduce speed | Driving the same speed as daylight |
| Passenger needs directions | Simplify prompts before the zone | Looking down at a phone in the work area |

## 1. Slow before the merge becomes urgent
The safest merge starts before the lane ends. Scan far ahead, reduce speed smoothly, signal early, and leave room for
late but non-aggressive merges. Night glare makes distance judgment worse, so assume other drivers may see cones and
lane shifts later than you do. The goal is traffic flow with margin, not proving who was first.

## 2. Create space for workers and equipment
Temporary work areas can include moving trucks, uneven pavement, people on foot, and equipment entering lanes. Increase
following distance and avoid pacing large vehicles through narrow lane shifts. If lane markings conflict with cones or a
flagger, follow the active traffic control rather than the faded line you expected.

## 3. Manage glare and passenger distractions
Clean the windshield, aim attention outside the vehicle, and keep passenger navigation simple. A bright phone, complex
conversation, or last-second route argument is more dangerous when the lane pattern is temporary. Ask passengers to read
directions before the zone or wait until the road opens up again.

## 4. Know when to exit the flow safely
If you miss a merge, see debris, or become uncertain, do not stop in a live lane or make a sudden cross-lane correction.
Continue with traffic until there is a lawful safe place to reset. Construction zones reward boring decisions: slower,
smoother, earlier, and more predictable.
