The first rain after a dry spell can make roads slick before they look dangerous. Oil, dust, worn tires, high speed, standing water, and delayed braking all reduce the margin. This guide was checked on 2026-06-14 against NHTSA, NOAA/NWS, FHWA, Ready.gov, and AAA resources. It is not legal or mechanical advice. Follow road signs, local law, police instructions, tire placards, owner manuals, and weather alerts first; never drive into floodwater.

## Wet-road and hydroplaning decision table

| Situation | Safer choice | Mistake to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| First rain begins after dry weather | Slow early and increase following distance | Waiting until the road visibly floods |
| Tires are worn or pressure unknown | Check or service before the trip | Assuming all rental or borrowed tires are fine |
| Water covers the road | Turn around and choose another route | Testing depth with the vehicle |
| Vehicle starts to skim | Ease off and steer smoothly | Braking hard or jerking the wheel |

## 1. Prepare tires before the storm, not inside it
Hydroplaning risk is closely tied to tire condition, inflation, speed, and water depth. Check tire pressure when cold,
inspect tread and damage, and replace unsafe tires before a long trip. Do not use a road trip as the test for tires
that already look questionable. If the vehicle is borrowed or rented, confirm the basics before leaving the lot or
driveway.

## 2. Slow earlier than instinct suggests
Speed is the easiest variable to control. Reduce speed before puddles, curves, lane changes, and bridges. Increase
following distance because braking takes longer and spray can hide hazards. Avoid cruise control in heavy rain and
keep both hands ready for smooth steering. The goal is not timid driving; it is preserving traction for the
unexpected.

## 3. Treat standing water and floodwater differently
A shallow wet lane and a flooded road are not the same problem. If water covers the road and depth is uncertain, turn
around. Floodwater can hide washouts, move vehicles, or disable them. Hydroplaning recovery advice does not make
floodwater safe. Use official weather and road information before and during the trip.

## 4. Recover smoothly if the car begins to hydroplane
If steering feels light or the vehicle skims, ease off the accelerator, avoid sudden braking, keep the steering wheel
pointed where you want to go, and wait for traction to return. Sudden inputs can make the skid worse. After control
returns, slow down and reassess whether the trip should continue.
